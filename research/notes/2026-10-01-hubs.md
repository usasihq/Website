## Decisions and judgment calls

- **Citing USASI's own methodology.** The chips hub had to say plainly that the catalog does not
  measure capacity, and the foundations hub that openness is assessed per artifact. Intro claims
  need sources, so I cite the live methodology page
  (unitedstatesofamericasuperintelligence.com/methodology/, fetched today). It states "does not
  publish ... power-capacity figures" and that an organization "is never labeled 'open'" because
  of one open-weight release. If the owner prefers hubs to cite only external sources, these
  sentences can move into `scope`, which needs no source.
- **Headquarters vs data centers.** Supported by the 10-K cover pages of NVIDIA (Santa Clara) and
  CoreWeave (Livingston, NJ). CoreWeave's 10-K separately describes data centers in the US, Europe
  and Canada, in leased buildings. I left out the 10-K's country count and all capacity numbers.
- **Memory figure.** In local-ai I quote one figure, from the gpt-oss-20b card: it runs within
  16 GB of memory under MXFP4 quantization, and all evals used that quantization. The precision is
  stated, as round-1 rules require.
- **Science access restrictions.**
  - MedGemma 27B has a Hugging Face gate (sign in and accept the HAI-DEF terms), and its card
    excludes direct clinical use.
  - ESM3, Evo 2 and ether0 document training-data or refusal measures, not access gates. I
    described these as such, not as restrictions.
  - Profluent-E1's attribution guidelines (FDA disclosure labeling) are noted as license
    conditions.
- **Provenance.** The science hub states that ether0 is fine-tuned from Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct-2501,
  a non-U.S. base model, so it does not look wholly U.S.-developed.
- **Words avoided.** No funding, valuations, user, staff or download counts, rankings, or
  superlatives. Vendor phrases ("state-of-the-art", "best-in-class", "superhuman") from the
  sources were not reused.
