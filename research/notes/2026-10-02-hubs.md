## Decisions and judgment calls

- **No scores anywhere.** The evaluation hub says what each benchmark measures, who verifies
  results, and how versions change. It quotes no results. The validator rejects the phrase
  "benchmark score", so the closing paragraph says "the scores that benchmarks produce". That
  sentence cites the live methodology page.
- **Hosted-only wording.** I did not write that Anthropic "does not release weights". The intro
  says only that it offers Claude as hosted services, which its models overview supports. The
  deprecations page notes that Anthropic "hopes to make past models publicly available again",
  which implies the weights are not public, but I did not rely on that.
- **OpenAI as "both".** openai.com/open-models returned 403, so I did not use it. Instead I cite
  the OpenAI API models page ("available via the Responses API") and the gpt-oss-120b card
  ("OpenAI's open-weight models", Apache 2.0).
- **GR00T N1.7 license inconsistency (stated in the hub).** The Isaac-GR00T README calls N1.7
  "fully commercially licensable under Apache 2.0". Its License section and the Hugging Face card
  both put the weights under the NVIDIA Open Model License. The robotics hub reports both
  statements and does not choose between them, in line with methodology §uncertainty. The
  `gr00t-n1-7-3b` record may need the same note. Its owner should decide.
- **openpi checkpoint terms.** The openpi LICENSE is Apache 2.0, and the `openpi` record applies
  it to code only. The repo also ships `LICENSE_GEMMA.txt` (Gemma Terms of Use) and does not say
  what it covers. The hub states both facts and does not say what license the checkpoints are
  under.
- **Agility safety claims.** These are framed as design statements. Two things support this: the
  press release says the safety architecture is "designed to reduce dependence" on barriers, and
  the product page says "certain safety features are still in development" and that safety
  features "do not eliminate all operational risk". I left out the press release's "first
  humanoid" and "first international safety standard" wording.
- **Provenance in enterprise-ai.** Writer's Palmyra-mini card says it is "Finetuned from
  Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B". The Qwen card's author field reads only "Qwen Team", so I did not name a
  company or country. I used the methodology rule that a U.S. fine-tune does not make its base
  model American.
- **DBRX.** The Databricks retirement table lists DBRX dates that have passed (pay-per-token
  2025-04-30, provisioned throughput 2025-12-19). This matches the archived `dbrx` records. The
  hub says only that the dates have passed.
- **The Pile.** The hub cites the Common Pile paper's statement that use of unlicensed training
  data "has previously resulted in DMCA takedowns of datasets such as the Pile". It does not
  describe current download status beyond what the `the-pile` record already says.
- **Words avoided.** No funding, valuations, revenue, user, staff, or download counts, rankings,
  or superlatives. I did not reuse vendor phrases from sources, such as "state-of-the-art",
  "most capable", "first", or "industry-leading". "only" appears just in its restrictive sense
  ("non-commercial use only").
