## Design choices

- **Evaluations.** The worked example is an annotated reading of Terminal-Bench's published methodology, framed around a hypothetical announcement. It has no invented numbers. The only figures are methodological facts from the sources: 89 tasks in version 2.0, at least five runs per pairing, 95% confidence intervals, and running the oracle five times. MLPerf and ARC-AGI (both published records) illustrate hardware availability categories, peer review, verified versus community results, harness labeling, and held-back sets. Draft records (SWE-bench, MMLU, GPQA) are not mentioned.
- **Training data.** The comparison uses two real documents: (A) the Olmo-3-1025-7B model card plus the Dolma 3 Mix card it links, and (B) the gemma-4-31B-it model card. Missing fields are written as "not disclosed in the reviewed source(s)". The text says explicitly that the Gemma 4 technical report was not reviewed and may say more, and that a gap is not a finding. No token counts or percentages are reproduced.
- **Ecosystem.** Real published records cover each layer: NVIDIA, Micron, AMD, and Intel (chips); CoreWeave and Microsoft (compute and hosting); Ai2 and Olmo 3 7B (models); PyTorch, the PyTorch Foundation, and vLLM (software); and Perplexity (applications). The roles of NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Google come from their catalog records. Non-members (TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix, contract manufacturers, Huawei Ascend, Rebellions, Cohere, DeepSeek, Mistral AI) appear only as context, described as "not catalog members". Their country is never stated, except for NVIDIA's own sourced statement that its supply chain is "mainly concentrated in Asia". The eligibility section restates the four bases and applies them to NVIDIA, vLLM, and Cogito v2.1 671B, matching each record's eligibility basis.

## Findings for editors (not fixed; outside this assignment)

1. **Terminal-Bench hosting wording changed.** The README footer now says Terminal-Bench "is hosted by Harbor and Laude Institute" and no longer names Stanford. The tbench.ai post footers still say "Hosted by Stanford / Harbor / Laude Institute". The `terminal-bench` record lists Stanford as a maintainer and links `stanford-university`. Its eligibility would still hold through Laude Institute and Harbor, but the maintainer list may need review.
2. **Olmo 3 7B dataset link redirects.** The model card's dataset link `allenai/dolma3_mix-6T-1025` returns 307 to `allenai/dolma3_mix-6T-1025-7B`, which is the URL the catalog already cites. That card now says the dataset is intended only for reproducing Olmo 3 7B and recommends `dolma3_mix-6T` for other uses. The `dolma` record could mention this.
3. **Nemotron page wording vs record.** NVIDIA's Nemotron page says the training data and weights "are open and available". The Nemotron release records note that some datasets require access approval. The explainer avoids the broader claim.
4. **Gemma 4 model card license link.** The card metadata still points to `ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/gemma_4_license`. That redirect was already reported in the glossary notes.
5. **ARC verification detail not used.** The policy says Verified Leaderboard scores come from a single run, with no averaging across runs, and caps run costs. This could be added to the `arc-agi` record's methodology or limitations note.
6. **SEC access.** EDGAR blocks the generic research UA. Agents that need filings should use WebFetch (as here) or the issuer's investor-relations copy, and must not add contact details to headers.
